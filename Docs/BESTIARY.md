# Bestiary: The Creatures of Jörmund

> *"Nothing on Jörmund is truly alive. Some things merely forget that they are dead."*
> *Skald Othlin, Hollowmere*

What follows is a record of every creature a newly arrived soul may encounter on the long shore, set down by those who walked it first so that those who walk it after need not learn every lesson in blood. Most are not monsters in the way the world beyond uses the word. They are what happens to a soul when it waits too long and the waiting hollows it out.

---

## The Ferals: Southern and Central Forests

These are the oldest dead on Jörmund. They arrived through the arches centuries ago, perhaps longer, and walked the shore toward the Life Pool as all souls do. But they never reached it. Some lost the path. Some were afraid. Some simply stopped walking and forgot why they had started. The forest grew over them and they grew into the forest, and now they are small, quick, cruel things with no memory of the names they carried before they died. They bleed black, same as any dead, but they do not know what the bleeding means.

### Kobolds

The smallest and most pitiful of the ferals. A kobold was a person once, perhaps a child, or someone who died very young and arrived on the shore with too little self to hold together. They band in threes and fours, dragging scavenged weapons they barely know how to use. Three varieties haunt the southern forest:

**Green Kobold.** Carries a sharpened stick it believes is a spear. It is not. It hides in undergrowth and strikes at ankles. The least dangerous thing on Jörmund, which is not the same as harmless.

**Brown Kobold.** Slightly older, slightly meaner. Wields a shortsword, a real one, likely pulled from a dead traveller's pack. Faster to anger than its green cousin, and harder to shake off once it gives chase.

**Blue Kobold.** The only kobold with the patience to use a bow. Stays back. Waits. Fires, and fires again. A nuisance alone; a genuine threat when three of them perch on a ridge while a green rushes you from below.

### Goblins

What a kobold becomes if it survives long enough and eats enough of the island's dim food. Taller, broader, sharp-eared. A goblin remembers nothing of who it was, but it remembers *how*: how to swing, how to aim, how to set an ambush. They are appallingly sensitive to noise; a fight with one draws every goblin in earshot. Spearmen, swordsmen, archers. They organise into rough warbands, though they lack the will to hold territory the way the king's forces do.

### Wolves

Not true wolves. These are souls that forgot even their human shape. They remember only hunger and the instinct to run toward warmth. A freshly arrived soul still carries a trace of the heat it brought from the world beyond, and the wolves will smell that trace long before they see you. Whatever form they have now, four-legged and grey-furred, is something the island gave them. Jörmund shapes the forgotten into what it needs.

**Pale Wolf.** The lunger. It does not circle. It does not test. It senses warmth and it closes the distance in a single surge, jaws first. Fast enough that hesitation is the same as being bitten.

**Grey Wolf.** Slower to commit but wider in its attack, a sweeping claw rather than a focused bite. Often found alongside pale wolves, which means the unlucky traveller must weather a lunge and a sweep at once. That is not an accident. They remember enough to hunt in pairs.

## The Shore: Western Beaches

### Carnifex

The carnifex are not dead souls. They are one of the few things native to Jörmund itself, creatures of the island's own tidal ecology, old as the arches, indifferent to the dead who walk among them. Massive, armoured in chitin the colour of old rust, with claws that close like millstones. They hear everything. A footstep on wet sand at fifty paces will turn a carnifex toward you, and once it turns, it does not turn away. The pincers hit slow but absolute, and patient souls learn the rhythm quickly. Impatient souls learn that chitin is thicker than it looks. The dead call them *carnifex* because that is what they are. Executioners. The beach is their court and every trespasser receives the same sentence.

---

## Sprekken: The Bone Crypt

Sprekken is not a natural place. It was carved out of the island's stone by the dead who chose to serve Hjarrandi rather than wait for the Pool. The king rewarded loyalty with purpose, a thing the dead crave more than anything else, and so they guard his crypt-roads even now, long after their flesh has fallen away and only the bones remain. They are not mindless. They remember *duty*. That is worse.

### Bone Flinger

The fastest of the crypt-guard. Strips a rib or a finger-bone from its own frame and hurls it with the accuracy of someone who has been practising the same throw for two hundred years. It keeps its distance, retreating the moment you close. Killing one is simple. Reaching one is the problem.

### Rib Hurler

Heavier, slower, and uninterested in precision. The rib hurler tears a fan of bones from its chest and throws them all at once, a spread that is easy to dodge at range and nearly impossible to dodge at close quarters. It replaces the ribs from a pile at its feet. The pile does not get smaller. No one has explained this.

### Bone Bulwark

The tank of Sprekken. It carries a shield made from a pelvis or a shoulder blade, and its frame is reinforced with stolen bones lashed on with sinew. It does not throw. It advances. It absorbs what you give it and waits for you to make a mistake. When you do, it fires a ring of bone shards in every direction, a last-resort eruption that punishes anyone who crowds it.

### Pillar Caller

The rarest and most unsettling of the crypt-guard. It carries a staff topped with something that glows a colour the dead should not be able to produce, a bruised violet, faintly warm. It does not attack directly. It raises pillars of bone from the crypt floor, reshaping the arena while you fight. The pillars block movement, funnel you into chokepoints, and occasionally erupt. Kill the caller first. Always.

## Brann: The Volcanic Shore

The western coast of Jörmund ends in fire. The arch there burns, and has burned since before the king's time. Brann is the oldest wound in the island's skin, a place where whatever lies beneath the long shore bleeds upward as heat and molten stone. The creatures here are not simply dead souls gone feral. They are souls that wandered too close to the wound and were *changed* by it. The fire got into them the way damp gets into wood. They are still dead. They simply burn as well.

### Ash Husk

The most common of Brann's warped dead. A human shape still, recognisably a person, but crusted in volcanic rock with ember-cracks running through the stone where fire still smoulders inside. They swing a cleaver of cooled rock that carries the heat of everything it has touched. Slow to wind up, wide in the arc, readable if you are paying attention. They are the first lesson of the volcano: learn the rhythm here, or learn nothing at all.

### Ember Sprite

Not a soul. At least, not a whole one. The sprites are fragments, slivers of identity that peeled away from souls consumed entirely by the fire. They remember nothing, not even hunger. They are heat looking for something to touch. Small, fast, fragile, and infuriating in groups. A single sprite is a nuisance. Four sprites while an ash husk is swinging at you is a crisis.

### Magma Salamander

Something worse than a feral. The salamanders were souls once, but the fire reshaped them completely: four legs, a low body, a jaw full of superheated fangs. They are drawn to residual warmth the way the wolves are, but faster, more aggressive, and more committed. A salamander does not circle. It lunges. The wind-up is short, the distance is long, and if you are not already moving when the lunge begins, you are already hit. The volcano's answer to the pale wolf, except the pale wolf does not set you on fire.

### Cinder Bloom

A growth, not a creature. Or perhaps both. The blooms root themselves in volcanic soil and swell with pressurised ash until the pods on their crown burst, lobbing chunks of molten material in high arcs that explode on contact. They do not chase. They do not need to. They turn the ground around them into a minefield of rolling fire, and anything that survives the pods still has to close the distance through scorched earth to reach the body. Functionally artillery. Treat them as such.

### Cinder Maw

The largest common creature on the volcanic shore. A quadruped built like a furnace on legs: low, wide, armoured in rock plates, with a gaping mouth that vents a sustained cone of flame. It is slow. Agonisingly slow. That is the trap. Travellers see the speed and assume they can ignore it. They cannot. The flamethrower's reach is longer than it looks, the damage is persistent, and the maw endures long enough to outlast the mistakes you make while learning these facts. Respect the wind-up. Stay out of the mouth. Kill the sprites and husks first, then deal with the maw alone.

## Sundust: The Eastern Desert

Beyond the coast, the island dries. The Sundust is the oldest region of Jörmund, the place where the first dead arrived, before the arches existed, before the Pool was tended. The sand is made of ground bone. Everything here has been waiting longer than anything should.

### Sand Worm

A soul so old it has forgotten it was ever shaped like a person. The worms burrow through the bone-sand and surface to strike, drawn by vibration. They are fast, sinuous, and surprisingly fragile for their size, a beast built for ambush, not endurance. Kill them before they burrow again or you will fight the same worm three times.

### Draugr Husk

The desert's version of a soldier. These are dead who arrived on Jörmund in armour and never took it off. The leather dried, the wrappings tightened, and the body inside became a thing of sinew and dust. They carry bows. They do not speak. Their eyes glow a sickly green that has nothing to do with fire and everything to do with the particular despair of someone who has been standing in the same place for four hundred years and cannot remember why.

---

## Hollowmere: The Northern Deep Forest

North of the central mountains, the forest thickens into something older and colder than the southern woods. Hollowmere is not corrupted the way Brann is. It is simply ancient, and the things that dwell in it have had longer to grow strong. The ferals here are not the pitiful kobolds of the south. They are what kobolds become if nothing kills them for a very, very long time.

### Hobgoblins

A goblin that survived long enough to grow into something worse. Taller than a man, tusked, dark-skinned as old bark. They fight in organised groups with real tactics: spearmen in front, swordsmen on the flanks, archers behind. They are what the southern goblins would be if the southern goblins had three centuries of practice. Respect the formation. Break the archers first.

### Orc

The end of the feral line. An orc was a person, once, and then a kobold, and then a goblin, and then a hobgoblin, and now it is this: a grey-green mass of muscle and fury that fights with a short blade and an absolute indifference to pain. It does not organise. It does not need to. It simply walks forward and hits whatever is in front of it until one of you stops moving. The only mercy is that they are rare.

### Brown Bear

Like the carnifex of the western beaches, the bears are native to Jörmund, not dead souls but island creatures. They have always been here. The northern forests are their territory and they do not share it with the dead or the king. Enormous, four-legged, with a hump of muscle across the shoulders and claws that leave gouges in stone. They are slow enough to outrun and fast enough that you cannot outrun them twice. The dead of Hollowmere give them a wide berth. You should do the same.

## Vassals

The dead use the word loosely. A vassal is anything that has claimed a place on the island and will not be moved from it. Some are loyal to the king. Some predate him. Some do not know he exists. What they share is territory, power, and an unwillingness to let you pass. They are listed here in the order a traveller walking from the South Arch toward the centre would encounter them, though few travellers make it past the third.

### The Skeleton King: Lord of Sprekken

The first and most loyal of Hjarrandi's vassals. He was a jarl when he drew breath, a lesser king who knelt to a greater one, and when he arrived on Jörmund he did what he had always done: he found the strongest man on the island and swore to serve him. Hjarrandi gave him Sprekken to guard, and he has guarded it for three hundred years, until his flesh fell away and only the crown remained. He fights with a broadsword and the muscle memory of a man who spent a lifetime at war. The crown is not decorative. He earned it, in his way. He simply earned it in service to the wrong king.

### Galgi the Bound: The Gallows-Knot

South of Wickbarrow there is a sunken grove where the trees lean inward and the air smells of old rope. Galgi is not one soul. Galgi is several, knotted together at the throat by a noose they tied themselves. They were afraid of the Pool. They believed that if they hanged together, bound tight enough, they could hold their shapes and never be unmade. It worked, after a fashion. They are still here, still bound, still screaming from several mouths at once. The knot writhes. The ropes lash. Cutting one free only makes the rest pull tighter. The horror of Galgi is not that it is strong. It is that every soul in the tangle chose this.

### The Drowned Crone: Keeper of the Net

She was a priestess of the sea in a life she barely remembers. When she arrived on the western coast of Jörmund she walked into the shallows and began to cast her net, and she has not stopped. The marshes around her are flooded with black water that rises when she is angry, which is always. Her net catches bones, driftwood, and anything foolish enough to wade within range. She fights from the centre of her flooded domain and the water itself is her weapon. The deeper you go, the stronger she pulls. The dead avoid the salt marshes entirely. There is nothing there worth finding except her, and no one wants to find her.

### The Cinder Sovereign: Warden of Brann

Not a servant of Hjarrandi. The Sovereign was here before the king, one of the oldest souls on the island, drawn to Brann's fire the way a moth is drawn to a lantern. The heat did not destroy it. It *became* it. Armoured in volcanic rock, crowned in undying flame, with a core of fire visible through the cracks in its chest. It guards the western arch not because anyone told it to, but because the arch is *its* fire, and it will not share. It cycles through flamethrower, lobbed pods, and a searing beam, and it summons lesser fire creatures when pressured. The Sovereign does not serve the king. It simply does not care that the king exists.

### Sindri's Apprentice: The Unfinished Work

In the mountains past Brann there is a forge that has not gone cold in three centuries. The apprentice stands at the anvil, hammer raised, sweat that should have dried a hundred years ago still beading on a brow of cracked stone. He was a smith's boy in the world beyond, apprenticed to a master he worshipped, and he died before he finished his masterwork. He has been trying to finish it ever since. The metal will not hold. The quench will not set. He does not notice. He hammers, and he hammers, and if you interrupt him he will turn the hammer on you with the full fury of a man who has been failing at the same task for longer than most kingdoms last. His seams glow white-hot when he strikes. The forge is his arena and he knows every inch of it.

### Gymir of the Hollow Mountain: The Sleeper

The mountain in the northwest is not empty. Gymir was a jötunn, or believed he was, or became one through sheer stubbornness and time. He was meant to sleep until the end of days, curled inside the stone the way a seed curls inside a shell. But the cycle stopped, and the days did not end, and he has been lying awake in the dark for longer than he can bear. His body is the colour of cave-stone, enormous, cracked down the chest where something inside him has gone hollow. He does not want to fight. He wants to sleep. He cannot. The rage comes from that, and it is bottomless.

### Skadi the Hoarfrost: The Girl Who Named Herself

She chose her name when she arrived on the shore, the way a child chooses a hero to pretend to be. She had been no one special when she was quick. On Jörmund she decided she would be Skadi, goddess of the hunt, and she spent centuries teaching herself to believe it. She is fast, cold, and precise. She fights first with a bow at distance and then with a lance when you close, and the transition between the two is the most dangerous moment in the fight. The ice around her is not natural. She made it. She made all of it, the frost and the wind and the persona, because the alternative was admitting she was just a dead girl on a shore with nowhere to go.

### Hrimfaxi's Get: The Mother and Her Young

In the woods outside Nasus, something larger than a wolf has made a den. Hrimfaxi's Get is a name the dead gave her because no one knows what she actually is. She is enormous, grey-white, four-legged, with eyes that reflect light that is not there. She fights alongside two smaller versions of herself. Kill the young and she howls, and the howl is the phase shift: she becomes faster, more erratic, more willing to throw herself into attacks that leave her open. She is protecting something. It is not clear what. There is nothing in the den but old bones and claw-marks on the stone.

### The Ash-Sprout: The Rotting Bough

In a caldera north of the mountain pass, something is growing. It should not be. Nothing grows on Jörmund except what was already here when the island was made. The Ash-Sprout is a bough of Yggdrasil, the world-tree, and it is dying. It fell from the canopy when the cycle stopped and it has been rotting in place ever since, half-alive, half-consumed by its own corruption. It does not move. It does not need to. The roots writhe, the bark splits, and the corruption sprays outward in arcs of black sap. At its centre, visible only when the bark is peeled back, there is something that might be a heartwood. Whether destroying it is mercy or murder is a question the dead have argued about for a very long time.

### Eir of Broken Wings: The Trapped Valkyrie

She should not be here. Valkyries do not come to Jörmund. They carry souls *to* the arches and then they leave. Eir did not leave. Her wings broke, or were broken, and she fell into the fortress on the northeast approach to the centre. She has been there since, unable to fly, unable to die a second time, pacing the halls of a ruin she did not build. She fights like something caged: vicious, desperate, with bursts of speed that no grounded thing should have. At the end, when her body fails, something pulls her back together. She does not want to be restored. She screams when it happens. But she gets up, and she fights again, because that is what a Valkyrie does.

### Heimdall's Echo: The Man Without a Name

The last guardian before the centre. He stands on the Crowned Bridge with a horn he has never blown and a shield he has never lowered. He had a name once. He gave it up when he swore to serve the king, and the king took it, and now he is only this: a shape in armour on a bridge. He calls himself Heimdall's Echo because the real Heimdall guards the bridge to Asgard and he guards the bridge to the tower. The comparison is a cruelty he inflicts on himself. He is not a god. He is a man who wanted to matter so badly that he let someone hollow him out and fill the space with purpose. He is very, very good at fighting. He has had nothing else to do.

### The Tyrant: He Who Sits at the Centre

No one speaks his name on the long shore. The dead know there is a king. They know he sits in the tower at the centre of the island, near the Pool, and that he has forbidden anyone from entering the water. Some say he was a great man once. Some say he was always this. The crypt-guard serve him. The bridge-warden serves him. The ferals and the beasts do not know he exists, and they are perhaps the luckiest things on the island for it.

He has been here longer than most of the dead can remember. The oldest souls, the ones who still have enough self to speak, say he arrived through the South Arch the same way everyone else did, and that he walked to the centre the same way everyone else tried to. He simply never left. What he found at the Pool, or what the Pool showed him, or what he refused when the water reached for him, no one knows. The tower was not there before he came. Now it is, and he is inside it, and the water does not move.

That is all that is known. That is all that needs to be known. The road leads to the centre. Whatever waits there has been waiting a very long time.

## Optional Challengers: The Eastern Fork

East of the main path, off the road to the centre, there are three creatures that guard nothing and serve no one. They are not part of the king's order. They are simply old, and powerful, and unwilling to be disturbed. Travellers who seek them do so for the kennings their defeat confers, not because the road demands it.

### The Giant Scorpion: Fang of the Sundust

The oldest predator in the desert. Not a dead soul but something that was here before the dead arrived, a creature of the bone-sand itself, armoured in chitin older than the arches. It guards nothing. It hunts because hunting is the only thing it has ever done. Two claws that close like gates, a tail that strikes from above, and a stinger that carries venom the dead should not be able to feel but do. It dwells beneath the sand and surfaces only when it is hungry, which is always.

### The Sandworm Broodmother: Heart of the Dunes

The largest creature in the Sundust. The broodmother is a nest, a coiled mass of segments with a tooth-ringed maw that opens like a flower made of razors. It does not chase. It surfaces, it screams, and smaller worms erupt from the sand around it. Kill the children and they return. Kill the mother and the desert goes quiet. The silence, they say, is worse.

### Gullveig Thrice-Burned: She Who Will Not Stay Dead

There is a cave on the eastern coast where the walls are scorched in three distinct layers of soot. Gullveig was burned three times in the old stories, and she came back each time. On Jörmund she has been burned more than three times, and she keeps coming back, and each return is angrier than the last. She fights in rounds. Put her down once and she rises wreathed in new fire. Put her down twice and she rises again, hotter, faster, smaller. The third time she stays down. She always stays down the third time. No one knows why she keeps choosing to come back when each return is worse than the one before. Perhaps she has forgotten how to stop.

*These entries are set down by the dead, for the dead. If you have arrived on this shore and do not understand where you are, read this, and walk carefully. The Pool is at the centre of the island. The road there is long. Not everything on it will let you pass.*
