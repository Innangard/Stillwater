# Jörmund — Story Bible

A working reference for the lore, tone, and structure of *Jörmund*. The aim here is to give the existing premise enough bones (gods, geography, factions, the king's identity) that every boss, NPC, and item can hang off a coherent skeleton — and to give the writing a Norse-skald voice consistent with the Observer's opening, which is already strong.

---

## 1. The Story

Jörmund is the long shore between life and death, a threshold where souls wait their turn to step into the Life Pool at the island's heart, to be unmade and returned to the living world to be born again. It was once a mercy, overseen by the Norns, three weavers who predate the gods. But the cycle was broken by Hjarrandi Eylifsson. In life, he was a warlord who unified coastal kingdoms and ruled them well for forty winters. In death, he arrived at the long shore, waited a century, and discovered the Pool's greatest secret: it runs entirely on consent.

When his name was called, the proud warlord refused. He demanded a worthier life and convinced other waiting souls to stand with him, eventually building a stone tower in the center lake where they could play king. Because no souls are unmade, no children are born, and the living world is slowly winding down. With no future left to weave, Skuld, the Norn of what shall be, has gone silent and hidden herself away. In a desperate final act, she reached across the threshold and pulled you down to the long shore — a thing only she could do, the gods being barred from this place. You are an anomaly: a living soul, awake, with red blood in your veins, stepping onto an island where the dead bleed only black ash.

You arrive as a blank slate. You have no history, and more importantly, you have no name. In Jörmund, names are power. To be known is to be strong; to forget who you were is to surrender to the primal terror of Ginnungagap, twisting into the feral, mindless monsters that roam the wastes. The titans of this island, the bosses you face, are those who held onto their identities so fiercely that they became twisted by them.

Your journey carries you across four ancient arches built by the gods. In the South, you learn the weary rules of the island from the ancient Observer and the gentle Bath-keeper Aud. You strike down the Skeleton King, an old fool who built a tomb of bones, and Galgi the Bound, a horrifying knot of souls who hanged themselves together to avoid losing their individuality. In the West, you push through the salt marshes and fell the Drowned Crone, a sea-priestess who refused to drop her net. You see a seedling of Yggdrasil rotting from the inside, a silent testament to the dying world. You vanquish the Cinder Sovereign on the burning western tip, then press deeper into the mountains past Brann to find Sindri's Apprentice still hammering at his unfinished masterwork — souls burning with an obsession for their domains.

In the freezing North, the tragedy crystallizes. You fight Gymir of the Hollow Mountain, a hollowed-out frost-jötunn meant to sleep until the end of days, and Skadi the Hoarfrost, a girl who spent centuries trying to become the goddess she was named after. You burn the Ash-Sprout in the caldera, a corrupted bough of Yggdrasil itself, and bring down Hrímfaxi's Get in the woods outside Nasus. It is here the Observer finally tells you Hjarrandi's name, revealing the warlord was once a good man. Pressing into the NE fortress and the Crowned Bridge to the Center, you fell Eir of Broken Wings, a Valkyrie trapped in a decaying fortress with her wings broken in her attempt to flee, and Heimdall's Echo, a man who surrendered his name to serve the king's delusion.

With every titan you slay, you do not just kill them; you take their Kenning. Because you have no name to protect, you absorb their whispered titles: Noose-Breaker, Ash-Walker, Valkyrie-Marked. You become a living vessel echoing with the myths of the souls you have laid to rest.

Finally, you breach the King's Tower. Hjarrandi, mutating into the serpent Níðhöggr under the weight of his hoarded eternity, hurls the very souls he trapped at you. Driven into the water, his monstrous form is rejected by the Pool, and you slay him in the shallows. Standing in the sudden quiet, with the water finally rippling to life, you are left with a single choice: step into the deep to return to the living world, or turn your back on the water, keeping your stolen titles and becoming the island's new, solitary warden.

## 2. Cosmology

### The Cycle
Souls die in the living world and arrive on Jörmund through one of the **four arches** — South, West, North, East — placed at the cardinal extremes of the island. They walk the long shore, sometimes for what feels like a season, sometimes for what feels like a hundred years. They eat (the food is dim but real), they sleep (sleep is the only forgetting). When their turn comes the Pool calls them and they step into it and are unmade and returned. **Birth in the living world is the Pool letting a soul go.** Without the Pool, no living woman bears a child, no foal stands, no salmon spawns. This is the slow horror behind the game's quest: the world the player came from is *winding down*, and the player may not have noticed because it hasn't been long enough yet.

### The Norns
Three weavers — **Urðr** (what was), **Verðandi** (what is), **Skuld** (what shall be) — tend the threads of every soul that lands on Jörmund. They are not gods. They predate the gods. They cannot leave the island either, but unlike the dead they were never alive in the first place. Only one of them — **Verðandi** — currently keeps a workshop in the player's reachable world, in Wickbarrow, where she presents to the dead as the local *fortune teller*. Urðr has gone quiet at her loom; Skuld has gone hidden entirely. The "fortune tellers" the player meets in Nasus (Svala) and Storsprunga (Ylla) are mortal souls who inherited the rune-craft from the Norns generations ago, when the Norns still walked openly. They are good practitioners. They are not Norns. *Fortune teller* is the in-world term for the trade; only Verðandi is the real article, and she does not advertise it. Skuld is the one who reached across the threshold and pulled the player down. The "gods" the Observer credits is the word the dead understand — there were no gods involved. There was a weaver with nothing left to weave.

### The black blood
When you die in the living world and arrive on Jörmund, you keep the shape of yourself but the substance changes. Your blood goes black because what runs in you is no longer life — it is *memory of life*, dark and slow. The dead can be hurt, killed, broken; their bodies dissolve into ash and reform overnight at the nearest arch they have touched. This is *Jörmund's* mechanic, not the player's. The player, being alive, bleeds red and dies for real. They get to come back only because Skuld is holding their thread carefully.

### The gods
Use the Norse pantheon by name where it earns the moment — **Odin**, **Tyr**, **Freyr** and **Freyja**, **Thor**, **Heimdall**, **Skadi**, **Frigg**. They are explicitly *outside* Jörmund and cannot intervene directly. They speak to the player only through omens: a raven, a one-eyed beggar on a road, a flash of lightning that strikes a tree the player needs felled. The four arches are **the gods' work** — they were built so souls could enter the island without being lost. The Pool itself is older than the gods, made by the Norns or by something before them.

### Yggdrasil
The world-tree on the small western island on the map — the one with the burned-looking arches around it — is a single *seedling* of Yggdrasil, grown from a cutting the Norns brought when they made Jörmund. Its roots reach down into the Pool — and into the dark beneath it, where the warlord's transformation has been quietly waiting. Níðhöggr in the old stories is the serpent that gnaws Yggdrasil's roots; here, that gnawing is Hjarrandi himself, lengthening and twisting in the deep. While the Pool flowed the tree thrived; now the Pool is dammed *and* the worm is already at the root, and so the seedling rots from two directions at once. The corrupted **Ash-Sprout** boss is one of its dying branches that has gone wrong. The seedling-tree being alive is one of the player's "the world is not yet lost" indicators throughout the early game; in the second half of the game it visibly begins to die, and every falling leaf is a quiet mark of the king's transformation.

### How souls go wrong

The bosses on Jörmund are not random monsters. They are all souls who refused the Pool, and they have all twisted in one of three directions:

- **Held the mortal name too tightly.** The Skeleton King, Galgi the Bound, Sindri's Apprentice. They could not bear to be unmade as the people they were. They built tombs, knots, and forges around themselves and stopped moving.
- **Took a god's name as a shield.** Skadi the Hoarfrost, Heimdall's Echo. They surrendered who they were *to* a borrowed identity larger than them, hoping the myth would survive what the self could not. This is the king's failure also: Hjarrandi is taking Níðhöggr's name without knowing it.
- **Hoarded a domain.** Drowned Crone with her net, Cinder Sovereign with her ash, Gymir with his mountain, Eir with her flight. They wrapped themselves around a single function until the function consumed them.

The player passes through all three patterns and absorbs a kenning from each. By the time they reach the king, they carry the shapes of every wrong way to refuse the water.

## 3. The Tyrant King

### Who he was
In life he was a warlord named **Hjarrandi Eylifsson** — a man who unified seven coastal kingdoms by the sword and ruled them well for forty winters. He was a *good* king by the standards of his world: just, generous in feast, hard in war. When he died he was old, content, ready. He arrived on Jörmund through the South arch four hundred years ago.

### What he became
He waited his turn at the Pool. He watched other souls — younger, less worthy by his measure — be called before him. He grew impatient. When his name came at last, he stood at the water's edge and *refused*. He had been a king. He would not be unmade and returned as a fisherman's child or a goat. He demanded a worthier life — and when the Pool would not bargain, he stepped back from the edge.

That alone would have been a long sulk, no more. But Hjarrandi was clever. He learned, over a century of refusing, that **the Pool runs on consent** — you walk into it of your own will, or it cannot take you. And so he began telling others to refuse too. To wait with him. To build him a hall where they could be kings together, all of them. They listened because he was charismatic and they were lonely. They built him a tower on the small island in the lake at Jörmund's center. They serve him because they have served him for so long they no longer remember a self that didn't.

### What he's becoming
This is where Fáfnir's story applies. Hjarrandi has hoarded eternity the way Fáfnir hoarded gold, and the hoard is reshaping him. Each year the king's body lengthens. His teeth move in his jaw. His skin grows scales beneath the silk. He keeps it hidden, even from his closest court. By the time the player meets him on the throne he can still pass for a man, but the transformation is nearly complete. Only the gods know, and they have only one piece on the board.

### The transformation
At low HP in the final fight, the king does what every monstrous figure in Norse myth eventually does: he stops pretending. His true form is **Níðhöggr** — the serpent that gnaws Yggdrasil's roots, the same roots the player has watched the western seedling rot above for the whole game. The crown falls. The throne breaks. He goes into the Pool and the Pool rejects him — it spits him back up enormous, scaled, and screaming. The final phase is fought in and around the water.

## 4. Geography (mapped to the player's journey)

Player route: **South → West → North → NE → Center.** The East mainland is an optional detour for kennings, off the main path.

| Region | Visual cue on the map | Story role |
|---|---|---|
| **South Arch** | Small dark arch at the very bottom | Player arrives here. Tutorial cliffs, the Observer NPC. |
| **Wickbarrow** | The southern fortified town | First hub. Smith Hela, Bath-keeper Aud, Merchant Vel, Notice Board. |
| **South barrows** | Implied — barrow-mounds south of Wickbarrow, reached via the eastern path out of town | Home of **Boss 1 (Skeleton King)**. |
| **The sunken grove** | A drowned copse south-west of the barrows | **Boss 2 (Galgi the Bound)** — the gallows-knot of cowards sharing one throat. |
| **Storsprunga** | Cluster of houses on the south-west coast | Western hub. Fortune-teller Ylla lives here. Harbourmaster. |
| **The salt marshes** | Yellow-brown wetlands between Storsprunga and the coast | **Boss 3 (Drowned Crone)** lives in the wrecks. |
| **The Yggdrasil-seedling isle** | Small western island with ruined arches and a tree | A pilgrimage site. Not a boss location — a *story* location. Visit and Skuld leaves a sign. |
| **Brann** | The orange-red volcanic arch on the far western tip | **Boss 4 (Cinder Sovereign)** — already built. |
| **The abandoned western forge** | A dwarven forge in the mountains past Brann | **Boss 5 (Sindri's Apprentice)** — still hammering at a masterwork he will never finish. |
| **The Hollow Mountain** | Stone peak in the NW | **Boss 6 (Gymir of the Hollow Mountain)** lives in the carved hall. |
| **The cave system** | Implied — between mountain and peak | **Brood of Surt** — three trolls, optional skip but heavily rewards. |
| **North Arch** | Dark arch at the top of the map | The North arch. A frozen peak; **Boss 7 (Skadi the Hoarfrost)** is its keeper. |
| **The northern caldera** | Round depression near the top-center | **Boss 8 (The Ash-Sprout)** — corrupted bough of Yggdrasil. |
| **Nasus** | The stone keep / city in the NE | Second main town. Fortune-teller Svala, blacksmith Keld. **Boss 9 (Hrímfaxi's Get)** lurks in the woods just outside. |
| **NE fortress** | The big castle on the upper-right peaks | **Boss 10 (Eir of Broken Wings)** — fallen Valkyrie. |
| **The Crowned Bridge** | The dotted boat-routes converging on center | **Boss 11 (Heimdall's Echo)** — the king's herald, gatekeeping the center island. |
| **The King's Tower** | The stone tower on the lake's island | **The Tyrant King → Níðhöggr.** |
| **The East** | The tan/dry mainland and blue eastern arch | **Three optional bosses.** Off the main path; available any time after the West arch activates. Reward kennings, not story progress. |

Boss count: **11 on the main path + the king = 12.** This hits the "around 12 before the king" target cleanly. Brood of Surt in the cave system is a heavily-rewarding optional fight, and the East mainland holds three more optional bosses off the main path. A completionist hunts 16 named fights total.

## 5. Tonal rules

The Observer dialogue is the calibration target. The voice is:

- **Plain. Old.** Short sentences with weight. "He grants it to no one. The water still ripples. Nothing leaves through it."
- **Tired, not despairing.** The dead have been here a long time. They are not screaming. They are weary.
- **Kennings, sparingly.** A character might say "whale-road" once for "the sea" — but not twice in the same conversation. Lean on them too hard and it becomes parody.
- **Names like riddles.** Hjarrandi means "screamer" in Old Norse — one of Odin's heiti, a god-name turned ironic in his mouth. Galgi means "gallows." Brisingr means "the burning one." Every named thing should mean something to a reader who looks.
- **The gods are never on-screen.** They are weather, omen, dream. The moment Odin actually shows up the game stops being about Jörmund and starts being about him.
- **Avoid the word "purgatory."** It is Christian. Jörmund is the *long shore*, the *waiting*, the *threshold*. Pick a phrase and let in-world voices use it.

### The Observer's voice — a sample for calibration

This is the voice every other character should be measured against. First meeting at the South arch:

> You are not like the others.
>
> I knew it before you came to the arch. The wind smelled wrong. The grass bent the way it bends only for the living.
>
> I have been here a long time. Four hundred winters, perhaps. I have lost count. I have watched souls arrive in their thousands and walk into the Pool when their names were called. That was the way of it, once.
>
> It is not the way of it now.
>
> A king crowned himself. He refused the water. He told the others to refuse with him. We listened, because he had a voice that filled the shore, and we were lonely.
>
> The Pool has not rippled since.
>
> I cannot tell you his name yet. You are not ready to carry it. Names here are weight. The dead who hold them grow strange. The dead who lose them grow worse.
>
> You will keep yours, I think. You have the only thing on this island worth having: blood that is still red.
>
> Go to Wickbarrow. The bath-keeper will wash the ash from you. The smith will look at you and know. The notice-board will tell you where the dead have gone wrong, and you will choose which one to face first.
>
> When the southern arch goes dark behind you, come back. I will give you what I know.

Note the rules at work. Sentences are short and almost all one clause. There are no kennings — he says "four hundred winters" once, and that is the only ornament he allows himself. He withholds Hjarrandi's name. He never explains the metaphysics; he describes only what he has seen. Other characters should sound like *people on the same island as this man*, with their own voices but the same air of weariness and the same restraint.

## 6. NPC consistency notes

The dialogue folder already has the right cast in skeleton form. A few small story-aligning suggestions:

- **The Observer** is implied to be very old — possibly one of the first dead to arrive, four hundred years ago. He may have known Hjarrandi as a man. He doesn't say so. (A late-game reveal could earn this.)
- **Verðandi** (Wickbarrow) is the only real Norn the player meets. She presents to the dead as the local *fortune teller* — that is the in-world term for the trade — but she names herself once the player has sat down with her. Her voice is patient, slightly out-of-time, never quite human. She speaks of her sisters openly and points the player toward the western seedling-isle as the place Skuld might have left a sign.
- **Fortune-teller Svala** (Nasus) and **Fortune-teller Ylla** (Storsprunga) are mortal souls who inherited rune-craft from the Norns generations ago, when the Norns still walked openly. They are not Norns and should never pretend to be. They cut runes that work because the old shapes still hold; they do not weave fate. Each acknowledges the inherited lineage in their own way — Svala wears it proudly, Ylla is plain-spoken and slightly dismissive of the family-myth. Players who pay attention to Verðandi's line about them will sort the real article from the inheritors. They should each, in addition, drop a one-line warning about the local boss in their region (Svala: Hrímfaxi's Get in the woods; Ylla: the Drowned Crone in the salt marshes) — they are local, they pay attention.
- **Bath-keeper Aud** is the gentlest NPC in the game. She washes the smell of black blood off the player. In-character she should be the one who first notices the player is alive — *"You smell wrong. You smell like rain on grass."*
- **Smith Hela** in Wickbarrow shares a name with the Norse goddess of the dead. She is not the goddess — she is a soul who took the name in life as a tribute and carried it across the threshold. She belongs to the second category (souls who took a god's name as a shield), but unlike Skadi or Heimdall's Echo, she took the name in humility, and so the twisting that ruined them has not yet ruined her. She has *opinions* about the king (his refusal mirrors a hubris she has spent centuries not committing herself). She is on the player's side and won't say why she knows so much. She forges weapons that bite the dead because she alone in Wickbarrow has thought clearly about what the dead are.
- **Merchant Vel** and the travelling merchants should be morally neutral. They buy and sell. They have no horse in the race.

## 7. The four arches as story beats

Each arch is a checkpoint *and* a tonal turn. The four-act structure of the game maps cleanly onto them:

| Arch | Act | Mood | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **South** (active at start) | I — Awakening | Curious, tutorial-warm | The player learns the rules. NPCs are friendly. |
| **West** (Brann region) | II — Heat | Defiant, mythic | The first real losses. The Yggdrasil-seedling visit. Skuld leaves her first sign. |
| **North** | III — Cold | Reverent, slow | The world is colder, sparser. Survivors here remember the king as a man. |
| **NE → Center** | IV — Reckoning | Sharp, urgent | The dead are restless. The tree visibly dying. The Pool stirring. Final push across the Crowned Bridge to the King's Tower. |

Activating the West arch should trigger a one-line system-wide change in NPC dialogue. The Observer, Verðandi, the fortune tellers, the smith — each gets a new line acknowledging it. (Mechanically the existing `arch_activated_<id>` story flag already supports this; the dialogue just needs the conditional pass.)

## 8. Visual motifs

A short guide for art direction. These are the recurring visual signals the player should learn to read.

### The blood signal
- **Red blood** = living. Only the player. It stains darker than the world wants it to and dries slowly.
- **Black ash** = dead. Every other shape on the island bleeds dark. Cuts smoke a little. Bodies dissolve into ash on death and reform overnight at the nearest arch.
- A pool of red and a pool of black side by side is the entire premise of the game in one frame.

### Palette by region
- **South** — pale grey-green coast, salt-bleached wood, ash-coloured stone. Light is overcast and even. Nothing is *dramatic*; the south is the calm voice that warns you what the rest will be.
- **West** — rust and ember. Volcanic black at Brann. The salt marshes between are sulphur-yellow and brown. Brann's heat does not move the air; it just exists.
- **North** — bone-white and slate. The Hollow Mountain is the only black silhouette against pale sky. Skadi's peaks have a blue cold to them that the South does not have.
- **NE → Center** — bruised purples and stone-greys deepening toward the King's Tower. The water of the central lake is the only still mirror on the island.
- **East (optional)** — dry tan, ochre, dust. The lightest region. Strong horizontal lines. The arches here are the freshest of the four, suggesting fewer souls have arrived through them.

### The sky
The sky over Jörmund does not move. It is a long pale afternoon that never tilts toward evening. Clouds exist but do not pass. *The sky changing colour is a final-act event.* When the Pool ripples again, the light shifts for the first time in three hundred years.

### Silhouette language
- **The player** is the only red-blooded silhouette. Their movement is faster than anything else on the island — the dead shuffle; the player sprints. This contrast is doing constant work.
- **The shuffling dead** are tired, slow, recognizably human, drably dressed.
- **The feral dead** (forgotten ones) move on all fours, jaws unhinged, eyes white. They are recognizably *what the shuffling dead become*. The visual gradient from one to the other should be legible.
- **The king's enforcers** wear armour that doesn't quite fit them — they took it from the warlord's hall and play at being soldiers. The wrongness should be visible: a too-large helm, a sword held badly. They are dressed up.
- **The bosses** each have one strong visual signature the player sees from far away — the Skeleton King's crown, Galgi's tangle of ropes, the Cinder Sovereign's halo of embers, Skadi's spear, Heimdall's stolen horn. These signatures appear in environmental detail before the fight: a discarded crown in the barrows, ropes hanging in trees, scorched footprints, a horn-call across the bridge.

### Recurring symbols
- **The crown.** Real, broken, or implied. The Skeleton King wears one; the king on the throne wears one; the central tower is a stone crown in shape. The player never picks one up.
- **The unbroken ripple.** Wherever water appears, it is unnaturally still. A single ripple is an omen. A second ripple is the climax of the game.
- **The scale.** Small glittering scales appear in environments touched by the king well before the final fight — caught on briars, in a torn piece of silk on the bridge, in the silt of the central lake. They foreshadow Níðhöggr.
- **The seedling tree.** Visible on the horizon from many regions. It is green in the early game and visibly browning by Act III. By Act IV its silhouette is bare against the sky.
- **The arches.** Each arch is the same shape but weathered differently. The South arch is the most worn. The East is the freshest. The Center has no arch — that is the point.

### The transformation visual
The king's transformation should not be a sudden flip. Throughout the game the player should see *foreshadowing scattered across the world*: scales on silk, a too-long shadow in a tapestry, a stained handprint on the throne that is too long-fingered to be human. The reveal at low HP is then the confirmation of something the player has been seeing all along.

## 9. Voices of the Long Shore

The lines below are a mix of forms — some are carvings the player finds etched into stone (the long ones, the formal ones, the prayers), some are journal scraps or scratched-out boards, some are dialogue overheard from background NPCs, some are barks. They are aggregated here by act for tonal consistency. When implementing, tag each as `carving`, `journal`, `bark`, or `overheard`; the writing was kept first-person across formats so the in-world voice stays uniform.

### Act I: The Awakening (South Arch & Marshes)

"We step onto this shore having left our bone-house behind in the dirt of the living world, yet we still feel the bitter cold of the sea wind. It bites at flesh that is no longer there, a phantom ache that never truly fades. You will look down at your hands and see the shape of them, but the substance has fundamentally changed into something heavy and dark."

"The water of the Pool does not pull you under by force; it only asks. That is the great, quiet cruelty of this island. You must step into the dark of your own free will, choosing to let the water wash the memory of your past from your mind. If the currents only dragged us, we would all be at peace, but instead, I stand shivering on this bank, completely unable to make the choice."

"Hold tightly to your name and speak it to the dirt every morning. When the mind-forge empties of who you were, the monsters take you, and you will forget how to speak entirely. I watched my brother forget his name yesterday; his jaw unhinged, his eyes went white, and he fled into the salt marshes to hunt for meat that will never fill him."

"We bleed black ash and slow memory. There is no slaughter-dew left in our veins to spill, only the dark resonance of the lives we once lived. When we are cut, the ash falls to the dirt, cold to the touch, and when we are broken, we simply dissolve and reform at the nearest arch."

"Three centuries ago, a fool in the south barrows refused the water before Hjarrandi ever arrived. He built a tomb of bones and sat in it, crowning himself sovereign of the dirt. He had no great charisma or followers; he was merely the first to show us how to fail."

"They feared the water so deeply they lashed themselves together with rope, choosing to hang from the gallows-tree rather than face the Pool alone. They became a horrifying knot of cowards sharing one throat, their bodies fused in a desperate bid to keep from being unmade. Now they swing in the sunken grove, a twisted mockery of sacrifice."

"She drags her heavy net through the shallows, endlessly searching the whale-road for men who have already drowned. In life, she served the sea goddess, and even in death, she refused to drop the net. Do not step into the water near the wrecks, or the sea-witch will pull you under to drown you again."

"I have moved this pile of stones from the left side of the path to the right, and then back again, every day for ten years. If I stop moving the stones, I will have to think about the stillness of the air, and if I think about the stillness, my mind will begin to fray. The stones give me a purpose when the gods have abandoned us to this waiting."

"They call this place a prison, but I was a thrall in the world above, beaten and starved by cruel masters. Here, the long afternoon never fades to black, and no man holds a whip over my back. Let the warlord keep his tower in the lake; I have found my paradise in the quiet dirt of the long shore."

"I grew so weary of the waiting that I threw myself from the southern cliffs, hoping to shatter my skull on the rocks below. I did not find the end I sought. My body simply dissolved into black ash, only to pull itself back together at the arch by morning. We are denied even the mercy of destruction."

"The bread we bake here fills the stomach, but it tastes of dust and old cobwebs. We eat only out of habit, going through the motions of the living to stave off the hollow feeling in our chests. I chew the tasteless crusts and close my eyes, trying desperately to remember the sweetness of wild honey."

"Sleep is the only true forgetting we have left on this wretched island. When I close my eyes, I am no longer trapped on this stagnant shore; I am back in my village, holding my daughter's hand. But every morning, I wake up to the same pale sky, and the loss cuts me deeper than any blade."

"I carve wooden boats from the driftwood I find along the southern coast, though there is no wind to sail them. I line them up on the sand, hundreds of them, pointing toward a horizon that never changes. It is a foolish task, but counting the boats reminds me that I have survived another year without losing my mind."

"For a warrior, this place is a quiet humiliation. There is no glory in striking down a shade that will simply reform at the arch tomorrow. We swing our swords out of boredom, pantomiming the great battles of our youth in a land where death has lost all of its teeth."

"I sit by the water and count the ripples, logging them in a ledger made of dried reeds. There are exactly four thousand ripples between the time I wake and the time I sleep. If the count ever changes, I will know the King has fallen and the world has begun to move again."

"I died of a wasting sickness when I was twenty, my body wracked with pain and rot. Here, my lungs are clear, my limbs are strong, and I will never age another day. The others weep for the world they lost, but I pray the water never calls my name. I want to remain exactly as I am."

### Act II: The Heat (West Arch & Volcano)
"A stranger walks among us, awake, and bleeding red. They do not belong on the long shore. Bath-keeper Aud says they smell wrong—like sweat and rain on grass, the scent of the living world. We are a graveyard of ash, yet the gods have dropped a beating heart into our stagnant waiting."

"The seedling on the western isle is rotting from the inside, its roots choked by the stagnant water. As the branches blacken and die, the living world above winds down. Without the flow of the Pool, no new children are born to their mothers. The warlord's pride is starving the earth we left behind."

"The corpse-sea in the center is dammed by the King's decree. It only ripples against the stone. Nothing leaves through it, and so the great cycle of the cosmos is entirely broken. The souls gather at the shores, staring at the still surface, knowing we cannot go home."

"The third weaver is gone. Because there is no tomorrow left to weave, she has fled into the dark to hide. Her sisters sit weeping in their workshops, tending to a past and present that lead nowhere. Until the King falls, the loom of the future remains violently still."

"He never finished his masterwork. Deep in the abandoned western forge, he hammers still, shaping a wound-hoe he will never swing in life. He was an apprentice to the greatest of dwarven smiths, and he refused the water because the metal was still hot. Now, the lava has fused with his cracking armor."

"The ash-weeper guards the Brann volcano, a queen of embers who burns all who dare approach her static reign. Like the Tyrant King, she claimed a domain to rule, hoarding the heat of the far western tip. She stands amid the pyres, scorching the flesh of any pilgrim trying to find the arches."

"The sky-candle does not move here. It is a long, grey afternoon that never ends, and we are so very tired of waiting. In the living world, the sun chased the moon, and the seasons turned. Here, the light simply hangs, mocking us with an eternity of twilight."

"I walked into the western marshes today just to feel the mud pull at my boots. It is a dirty, foul-smelling place, but the stench of sulfur and rot is the closest thing to reality I can find. The pristine, sterile ash of the safe paths was driving me completely mad."

"My husband arrived at the West arch a century after I did. We wept, holding each other, swearing we would never be parted again. But yesterday, the Pool called his name, and he walked into the water to be unmade. I am alone again, terrified that my turn will never come."

"I write my sins in the volcanic ash near Brann, hoping the heat will burn them away. But there is no wind to scatter the dust, and the rains never come to wash the ground clean. Everything you leave behind in this place stays exactly where you put it, forever."

"They say the King built his tower out of fear, but I understand him. Why should we surrender the minds we cultivated, the skills we honed, just to be wiped clean like slate? The King is the only one who realized that eternity does not have to be an erasure."

"I cannot remember the face of my mother. I try to draw her features in the dirt, but the lines always blur into the face of the feral beasts roaming the edge of the camp. Memory is a sieve, and the longer we wait, the wider the holes become."

"I sought out the giant in the hollow mountain just to see something massive, something awe-inspiring. He nearly crushed me with a boulder. It hurt terribly, but as my body dissolved into ash and sent me back to the arch, I smiled. It was the most exciting thing to happen to me in eighty years."

"We gather near the western tree to watch the leaves turn brown and fall to the earth. It is the only thing on this entire island that changes. We treat every dropping leaf as a sacred event, a reminder that time is still passing somewhere, even if it has stopped for us."

"I saw the living stranger bleed today. The liquid was thick, bright crimson, and it stained the stone where it fell. A feral shade tried to lick it from the ground, whimpering like a starved dog. The sight of red blood in this world is a terrifying, beautiful omen."

"I sit in the shallow hot springs near the forge to thaw the chill from my bones. The heat is an illusion; my real bones are buried under a frozen tundra in the world above. But if I close my eyes and let the steam rise, I can almost pretend I am resting after a long hunt."

### Act III: The Cold (North Arch & Peaks)
"The giant in the hollow mountain awoke when the cycle broke. In life, he was hammered into the stone by Odin himself, meant to sleep until the end of days. But Jörmund is fractured, and so he rose. He is empty inside, carrying nothing but the cold in his cavernous chest."

"We are trapped against the ice-bone of the northern peaks. The frost takes our fingers, blackening the tips of our hands, but we cannot die to escape it. We huddle together for warmth that our bodies no longer produce. The King promised a kingdom, but all he gave us was an endless winter."

"She died at sixteen. She hunts forever with a winter-spear, playing at being a goddess in the snow because her own life was stolen too soon. For two centuries, she has run across these frozen peaks, demanding that the lost souls of the island call her Skadi."

"He was a good king once. A generous ring-giver who united the seven coastal kingdoms by the sword and ruled them well for forty winters. When he arrived through the South arch, he was an old man, content and ready for rest. It is a tragedy he twisted into a monster."

"When his turn came, he stood at the water's edge and refused. He had been a warlord. He would not be unmade and returned as a fisherman's child or a goat. He demanded a worthier life, and when the Pool would not bargain, he stepped back."

"The great moon-hound feeds her pale pups on the bodies of lost pilgrims in the snow. She stalks the moonlit woods outside Nasus, a massive, scarred beast protecting a feral brood. We are either mindless prey, or monstrous predators surviving on the ash of our kin."

"The madness takes the forgotten souls like a fever, tearing their minds apart until only the fear of starving remains. First, they forget the faces of their children. Then, they drop to all fours in the mud, howling at a sky that offers no answers."

"I walk the perimeter of the North arch, waiting for my son to arrive. He was only a boy when I died of the fever. I have waited seventy years, and every day I pray that he does not appear, for his arrival would mean his life has ended."

"I refuse to sleep. The moment I close my eyes, I feel the freezing water of the lake where I drowned filling my lungs all over again. The others tell me that sleep is the only escape, but for me, it is a nightly return to my most agonizing moment."

"We built a shelter from the frozen pine branches, huddling beneath it like frightened children. The weather never changes, the snow never falls heavier than a light dusting, yet we cling to the illusion of a roof. We need the walls to pretend the world has boundaries."

"I was a priest who dedicated my life to the Allfather. I burned offerings, chanted the runes, and spilled the blood of our enemies in his name. To arrive here and realize the gods cannot even cross the threshold of this island... it broke my faith entirely."

"I follow the wolf tracks in the snow, hunting the beasts not for their meat, but just to remember what it feels like to hold a bow. The string snaps against my frozen wrist, a sharp, sudden pain that reminds me I still exist. I let the pups live, just so the hunt never truly ends."

"I spend my days talking to a large, grey stone near the caldera edge. I have named him after my old hunting dog. The stone does not judge me when I weep, and it does not try to convince me to build a hall for a tyrant. It is the best companion on this wretched shore."

"I challenged one of the King's armored guards today. He ran me through with a halberd, and my body turned to black ash on the snow. I reformed at the arch, picked up a heavy branch, and went back to fight him again. It is a pathetic rebellion, but it is mine."

"The silence of the snowy peaks is maddening. The living world was so loud—the crashing of waves, the shouts of men, the clatter of iron. Here, the snow absorbs every sound, leaving me alone with the deafening roar of my own fading memories."

"I collect the discarded, rusted armor of those who have given up, piling it in a cave near Nasus. One day, I will forge it all together into a great shield. Not to fight the King, but simply to hide behind when the madness finally comes to take me."

### Act IV: The Reckoning (NE Approach & Center Lake)
"This living stranger bleeds red. The hot, coppery scent drives the feral ones mad. The monsters have tasted nothing but cold ash for centuries. They swarm the stranger, desperate to consume the one thing they can never possess again."

"She tried to fly off this island, but the gods could not catch her. Her wings snapped, and now she hates the living. She is trapped in a decaying stone fortress, and when she falls, her bitter wrath forces her to rise again."

"He guards the bridge with a horn, a loyal feeder-of-ravens who abandoned his own name to serve a tyrant's delusion. He donned plate armor, grabbed a great sword, and called himself Heimdall, standing upon the causeway to parry every strike."

"We thought we left the spear-din behind in the living world. We thought the long shore was a place of peaceful transition. But here, the dead butcher the dead, dissolving into black stains on the earth, only to rise the next day and fight the exact same battles."

"We built him a stone tower on the center lake so we could play kings together. We listened because he was charismatic, and we were so incredibly lonely. We did not know what the hoarding of eternity would do to him, or the world."

"I saw his skin beneath the silk. The King is gone. He is a root-gnawer now, lengthening and twisting in the dark. When the crown falls, you will see the serpent he has become, waiting to poison the very water he refused to enter."

"The wave-swine rot in the eastern shallows, their ribs jutting out from the sand. They bring no new souls, only the silent, coiled spawn of the world-serpent. The child-beast spent centuries growing in the wrecks, completely lethal to anyone who wades too deep."

"The valkyries promised us an endless harvest of fallen heroes, but there is no door to the hall they spoke of. We were told to fight bravely to feast with the gods. It was all a lie. We are permanently trapped in the waiting room."

"The lake is still as glass. If the living vessel strikes the serpent down, the water will finally ask them the same question it asked the King centuries ago. Will they return to the living world, or become the new warden of this terrible shore?"

"I sit in Bath-keeper Aud's shallow pools from the moment I wake until I sleep. The water is tepid, but she washes the smell of the black blood away with such gentle hands. It is the only touch on this entire island that does not feel like a threat."

"Sometimes I hold my breath until my lungs burn, sinking to the bottom of the eastern shallows. I know I cannot drown again, but the desperate, panicking thrash of my own body is the only way I can force myself to feel truly alive."

"I begged the fortune teller in Wickbarrow to weave me a new thread, to give me a destiny outside of this stasis. She only looked at me with ancient, pitying eyes, and told me her real name. She told me she weaves the now and only the now, because her sisters are gone — and that there is no future left to give me, because no future is being woven anymore."

"I arrived a week ago, and I still believe this is a fever dream. The sky is too pale, the dirt is too dark, and the people here speak in riddles about a water that asks for your consent. I will wake up in my own bed soon. I must."

"I weave grass baskets from the reeds along the lake, tying them tight and sturdy. As soon as I finish one, I tear it apart with my bare hands and scatter the grass into the wind. Creation and destruction, held entirely in my own control. It is my only power here."

"I wonder what happens after the Pool pulls you under. Do you retain any spark of who you were, or are you wiped entirely clean, poured into the vessel of a newborn infant? The fear of that total erasure is why the King's lie sounded so sweet to us."

"I am terrified of forgetting my own face. I stare into the still waters of the center lake, looking at my reflection for hours. If I blink too long, the face looking back at me seems like a stranger. The water is trying to unmake me before I even step in."

"I was a king myself, in a small valley in the north. I died with a crown on my head, betrayed by my own brother. Here, I am nothing, and I am glad for it. Let Hjarrandi bear the weight of ruling a graveyard; I am finally free of the throne."

"The King stole the choice from us. The Pool runs on consent, but his enforcers block the paths and demand our loyalty before we can even reach the shore. He did not save us from the water; he made us hostages to his own crippling fear of being forgotten."

"If eternity is a circle, then we are the dirt it is drawn upon. We are trampled, shifted, and turned to ash by the passing of the ages, yet we can never escape the shape of the ring. The living vessel is the only thing capable of breaking the line."

"I watched the witch on the eastern plateau burn to ash and rise again, radiant and golden, defying the very laws of this threshold. She has survived three deaths, a testament to pure, stubborn existence. If she can refuse to fade, perhaps I can too."

"The hound in the coastal cave howls with a sound that chills the marrow. The noise does not come from its throat, but from the hollow wind blowing through its empty chest. It is the sound of absolute loss, echoing across the forgotten branch of the island."

"I am a coward. I ran from every battle in my life, and I died with an arrow in my back. I expected the searing fires of punishment. Instead, I found a quiet shore where no one asks anything of me. For a craven like me, this eternal stagnation is a profound mercy."

"I write poems in the dirt near the Crowned Bridge, knowing the King's armored boots will eventually trample them into dust. The destruction of my art does not matter. The act of writing proves that my mind still belongs to me, not to the Warlord in the tower."

"I sit near the center lake and watch the ash-blood dry on the stones. It turns from a wet, glistening black to a dull, powdery grey. It is a morbid fascination, tracing the outline of where a soul temporarily dissolved before pulling itself back together."

"I have forgotten if I was a husband or a wife. I have forgotten the name of the god I prayed to. I exist now only as a watcher, a hollow vessel sitting on a ridge, waiting for the sky to change color. I am not a monster yet, but I am no longer a person."

"The living stranger moves with a terrifying urgency. We shuffle through our centuries like sleepwalkers, but this anomaly sprints, dodges, and strikes with the desperate speed of something that knows its time is finite. They are a blur of red in a world of grey."

"I pity the feral ones. We curse them and hunt them when they wander too close, but they are only victims of the waiting. The King dammed the water, and the stagnant time rotted their minds. Every beast you strike down is a crime laid at Hjarrandi's feet."

"I tried to weave a net to catch the silver fish in the lake, but the water is too still, and there is nothing swimming beneath the surface. The island provides dim food to keep us from starving, but it refuses to let us hunt or gather for ourselves."

"The King's herald blew his great horn upon the bridge, the sound echoing across the mainland. It was not a call to battle; it was a desperate demand for attention. They are playing dress-up in the ruins of the cosmos, terrified that the audience is leaving."

"I found a piece of silk torn from the King's robes, caught on the briars near the causeway. It was stained with a foul, viscous residue, shedding small, glittering scales. The sovereign we bow to is molting in the dark, shedding his humanity piece by piece."

"They say the living soul will face the King in the water. I do not care who wins. I only care that when the violence ends, the water begins to move again. I am so tired. I am so ready to let the memory of this place wash away."

"If you are reading this, know that Jörmund was a kindness, once. It was a gentle shore between the pain of dying and the pain of being born. It was the hubris of a single man that turned a sacred threshold into a cage. Do not let him keep the key."
